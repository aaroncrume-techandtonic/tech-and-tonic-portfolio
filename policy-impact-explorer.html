Executive Context & Policy Tensions

Following the arrival of executive administrative changes at the coastal enterprise, a strict zero-tolerance Team Member Alcohol Consumption Policy was posted. While intended as a standardized corporate risk-management tool, Section 3.b extends off-duty alcohol restrictions to scheduled days off and guest visits across all enterprise venues (Casino, Hotel, and Fuel Mart). In an isolated rural coastal community where the casino serves as the primary dining, gathering, and entertainment hub, this blanket prohibition has created severe friction, morale drops, and loss of key talent.

🏙️ vs 🌲

The Urban vs. Rural Disconnect

In a major metropolitan area (e.g., Las Vegas or major cities), an off-duty employee has hundreds of alternative dining, bar, and recreation options within minutes. In a small rural coastal town, options are extremely limited. The casino enterprise is not just a workplace—it is the central social hub for families, neighbors, and colleagues. Banning off-duty visits severs community cohesion.

Key Insight: Standard corporate playbooks imported without local adaptation turn security and surveillance into monitors of their own neighbors, destroying trust.
🛡️ vs 🚫

Blanket Prohibition vs. Conduct Leadership

Blanket Prohibition treats 100% of staff as inherent liabilities, punishing responsible adults to manage the potential mistakes of 1%. Conduct-Based Leadership sets clear expectations: strict zero tolerance for on-duty impairment, but treating off-duty staff as valued adult guests bound by standard conduct rules.

Strategic Goal: Shift from punitive venue exclusion to behavioral accountability aligned with tribal values of kinship and dignity.

Why Blanket Prohibition Fails in Rural Settings

  • Small Labor Pool: Replacing experienced local staff takes months and inflates recruitment expenses.
  • Loss of F&B Revenue: Off-duty staff are frequent, loyal patrons of company restaurants and lounge events.
  • Breakroom Whispers: Bans on dining lead directly to fears of gaming bans, eroding goodwill.

The Conduct-Based Solution Advantage

  • Target Bad Behavior, Not People: Discipline individuals who disrupt guest areas while respecting the rest.
  • Civilian Attire Mandate: Requires off-duty staff to remove uniforms/badges, keeping professional separation.
  • Restorative Accountability: Upholds enterprise protection without sacrificing community kinship.

From the Headwaters to the Coast: Leading with Indigenous Kinship, Not Blanket Restrictions

Audience: Tribal Council, Executive Leadership, HR Director, Department Heads

"Members of the Tribal Council, Executive Leadership, Human Resources, and my fellow managers and colleagues..."

I stand before you today not just as an employee of Lucky 7 Casino & Hotel, but as a proud Klamath and Modoc person whose ancestors thrived at the headwaters where our rivers begin.

My ancestors understood a fundamental truth: what happens at the headwaters impacts everything downstream until it reaches the ocean. The waters connect us. From the lakes and headwaters of Klamath country down through the river canyons to this rich North Coast land of coastal nations, our indigenous peoples share a common thread. We share a history of resilience, a deep devotion to family and community, and an ongoing journey toward self-determination.

That is why I care so deeply about this enterprise. Tribal enterprises were not created merely to mirror cold, outside corporate machines. They were built on tribal sovereignty—to uplift our families, support our local people, and strengthen our community.

Today, we stand at a critical crossroads. With the arrival of new management, we recognize the intent to bring structure and risk management. But leadership cannot happen in a vacuum, isolated from the land, the culture, and the people it governs.

"What works for a mega-resort on the Las Vegas Strip or a massive corporation in a densely populated city DOES NOT WORK here on the North Coast."

Since these new off-duty alcohol policies were posted, morale in our break rooms has plummeted. Workers who pour their hearts into keeping our guests returning now feel distrusted and watched on their days off. We have already lost several key, veteran employees—people with institutional knowledge who walked out because they felt degraded by a blanket policy.

Let us look at the difference: A blanket prohibition assumes every employee is an inherent risk, punishing ninety-nine percent of responsible adults. A conduct policy honors individual responsibility and tribal values: 'If you report to work impaired, you are disciplined. If you cause a disruption off-duty as a guest, you are held accountable.'

From the headwaters to the ocean, our strength has always been our people. Let us protect our enterprise by honoring and standing by our people first.

SUBJECT: Operational & Socio-Economic Review of Off-Duty Enterprise Alcohol Restrictions

This message is brought forward out of profound care for the financial health, reputation, and community standing of this coastal enterprise. Tribal enterprises were created to uplift families, support local workers, and strengthen regional economic self-reliance.

The indigenous peoples of this region have always recognized that headwater management affects the entire downstream ecosystem. Today, recent administrative policy shifts introduced to manage corporate liability have generated unintended operational friction.

"In an isolated rural coastal setting with limited recreational venues, banning off-duty staff from utilizing the facility as guests severs community cohesion and damages employee retention."

Operational Impacts Observed: Breakroom morale has dropped significantly, and whispers regarding future restrictions (such as potential gaming bans) are circulating. Crucially, several veteran department staff have resigned, degrading operational capability in a tight labor market.

Recommended Resolution: Replace blanket facility prohibitions with a robust, conduct-based policy framework. Maintain absolute zero-tolerance for on-duty impairment while allowing off-duty staff in civilian attire to patronize public amenities under standard guest conduct standards.
